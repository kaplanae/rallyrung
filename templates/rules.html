Pickleball Doubles Ladder Rules

The Cedar Park Pickleball Doubles Ladder is FREE for players 18 and over. It's a fun, competitive way to meet local players and play doubles matches on your own schedule — no required match days or weekly commitments. Read the rules below before signing up.

How It Works

  • All teams are ranked and listed on the ladder.
  • When you join, you are added near others with a similar playing level.
  • Each month, teams are grouped in 3's according to rank. During that month, your team schedules and plays two matches against the other two teams in your group.
  • You have the entire month to play your 2 matches. Contact your opponents within the first week of the month. Text is best, but email or a phone call works too.
  • Some months a group may only have 2 teams, giving one team a bye.
  • If your team fails to play any matches for 2 straight months, you will be automatically dropped from the ladder. You can rejoin the following month from your Profile page.

Partner Selection

  • Each month, you select a doubles partner from the active players on the ladder.
  • Go to My Group and use the partner request feature to invite someone. They must accept your request to be confirmed.
  • Once confirmed, you and your partner are a team for that month. You play together against the other teams in your group.
  • You may change your partner before your matches are played. Either partner can cancel the partnership from My Group.
  • Partners do not have to be the same people month to month — mix it up!

Match Locations

Players arrange the dates, times, and locations of their matches. Any mutually convenient local pickleball court works. Be on time — arriving 5 minutes early is the standard. If you're running late, notify your opponents as early as possible.

Match Format

The match format is best of 3 games. Each game is played to 11 points, win by 2. If teams split the first two games, a third game is played to 11, win by 2.

Players can agree to modify the format, but the above is the default if no agreement is reached.

Serving Rules

  • Only the serving team can score points. If the receiving team wins a rally, they earn the serve but not a point.
  • Each player on the serving team serves until they commit a fault, except at the start of the game when only one player serves before the serve passes to the other team (the "first server" exception).
  • The serve must be made underhand, with the paddle below the wrist and the ball contacted below the waist.
  • The serve is made diagonally crosscourt, landing in the opposite service box beyond the kitchen (non-volley zone).
  • The server must keep both feet behind the baseline when serving. At least one foot must be on the ground when the ball is struck.
  • If a serve hits the net and lands in the correct service box, it is a let — replay the serve.

The Kitchen (Non-Volley Zone)

  • The kitchen is the 7-foot area on both sides of the net.
  • You may not volley (hit the ball in the air before it bounces) while standing in the kitchen or while your momentum carries you into the kitchen after a volley.
  • You may enter the kitchen to play a ball that has bounced inside it.
  • You must exit the kitchen before volleying again.

The Two-Bounce Rule

After the serve, each team must let the ball bounce once before hitting it — the receiving team lets it bounce on the serve, and the serving team lets it bounce on the return. After these two bounces, either team may volley or play off the bounce.

Common Faults

  • Hitting the ball out of bounds
  • Hitting the ball into the net
  • Volleying from the kitchen or stepping into the kitchen on a volley follow-through
  • Volleying the serve or return of serve (two-bounce rule violation)
  • The serve lands in the kitchen or on the kitchen line
  • Touching the net or post during play

Penalties for Late Teams

  • If a team arrives late, the opposing team may apply a point penalty: 1 point per 5 minutes late.
  • Penalties begin at the scheduled match time.
  • The match is a forfeit at 15:01 minutes late.
  • If a team arrives before the forfeit time, the late team is to receive a 5-minute warm-up.
  • Late penalties apply only when the late arrival is the fault of the arriving team. Exception: courts not available at scheduled time.

Score Reporting

After the match, both teams should report the score right away under My Group. One player from each team can report. Possible outcomes:

  • Completed — The match was played with a winner and final game scores.
  • Schedule Problem — Both teams tried to find a time but couldn't.
  • Weather Problem — Weather prevented the match.
  • Injury (match not finished) — The match started but one team had to retire due to injury. The non-retiring team gets the win, and partial scores are reported.
  • Injury (match not played) — The match never started due to injury or illness. The other team gets the win.
  • Forfeit — One team made no effort to schedule, refused to play, or failed to show up. The other team gets the win.

Monthly Rankings

At the beginning of the next month, the team with the best record in the group moves up to the next group, and the team with the worst record moves down. The middle team stays. Ties in match wins are broken first by head-to-head record, then by games won vs. games lost.

Partners move together — both players in a team gain or lose ranking together based on the team's result.

If you change partners month to month, your individual ranking carries over to your new pairing.

Sportsmanship

This ladder is competitive but friendly. Make calls honestly — if you're not sure, give the benefit of the doubt to your opponents. Be on time, call the score before every serve, and report results promptly. It's OK to lose!

Ball Recommendations

Use an outdoor pickleball approved for competitive play (e.g., Dura Fast 40, Franklin X-40, or similar). Both teams should bring a new ball. The winning team takes the unopened ball; the losing team takes the used ball.

Texas Tennis Ladders

Texas Tennis Ladders are FREE for men and women 18 and over and are a way for local tennis players to meet and play matches on their own schedules without having to make any commitments to play regularly scheduled matches. Make sure that you read and fully understand the rules below before signing up.

How It Works

  • All players are ranked and listed on the ladder.
  • When you join you are added near others with similar tennis ratings and must work your way up.
  • You can leave the ladder at any time, and rejoin at any time. To leave the ladder, go to your Profile page on the website and click Leave Ladder. When you rejoin the ladder you will be added at a similar ranking to where you left, or below.
  • Each month players are grouped in 3's according to rank and some time during that month you schedule and play your two matches with the other two players in your group. This doesn't mean that all three have to meet at the same time and play two back to back matches. It means that if I am player 2, I get in touch with player 1 to schedule our match some time during the month and I also get in touch with player 3 to schedule a match during the month.
  • You have the entire month to play your 2 matches, but try not to wait until the last day. You should contact your opponents within the first week of the month. Text is best, but if you get no response don't forget about email or an actual phone call with voices.
  • Depending on the number of players each month, there may be groups in a month that only have one match and a bye for the second match.
  • If you fail to play any matches for 2 straight months (nothing reported or forfeit) you will be automatically dropped from the ladder. You will be off the ladder for the remainder of that month but can sign up again starting the following month from your Profile page.

Match Locations

Players arrange the dates, times and locations of their matches. Please be respectful of other people's time. On-time for your match should be 5 minutes early.

Approved court locations are specific to each ladder. Please select a specific ladder location to see the list of approved courts.

Match Format

The match format will be the best two out of three sets, with the third set being a 10 point tiebreaker if needed. Players can agree to modify the match format for the situation, but if there is no agreement on something different, then the format mentioned above is the format. For example, if players are having a great match and are feeling good and want to do a full third set, they can agree to that.

Penalties for Late Players

  • The point penalty system is enforced with the late player losing toss plus 1 game for each 5 minutes late.
  • Penalties begin at the scheduled time of the match.
  • The match is a default at 15:01 minutes late & considered a 6-0, 6-0 win for the player/team present.
  • If the player arrives before the defaulted time, he/she is to receive a 5 minute injury prevention warm-up.
  • Late penalties will be imposed only when a player's late arrival is at fault for holding up the start of play. The only exception is if courts are not available at the match scheduled start time(s).

Example: Match start time is 8:00.
8:01-8:05 = loss of toss, plus 1 game.
8:06-8:10 = loss of toss, plus 2 games.
8:11-8:15 = loss of toss, plus 3 games.
8:15:01 = Match Default.

This means that you should be early for your match. If you schedule your match for 7:30 pm, then you should be there before that time.

We all know that sometimes life happens and things come up. If you know that you are not going to be on time, contact your opponent and give them as much notice as possible. If this happens and the players agree to a new time then the penalties don't start until that new time.

Time Between Points, Games, and Sets

  • 20 seconds between points
  • 90 seconds on changeovers (odd games)
  • 120 seconds at the end of each set
  • There is no break after the first game of any set, so players change sides right away but can get a quick drink without sitting
  • Drinks should be taken on odd games only

Tiebreakers

USTA rules use the COMAN tiebreak both to determine the winner of a set and a match. This is the format we use unless both players would rather play a traditional tiebreaker switching sides every 6 points.

  • When the set score is 6-6, the player or team who last received will serve the first point of the tiebreaker from the deuce side.
  • Thereafter, each player shall serve in rotation for 2 points, starting from the ad (left) side of the court, then the deuce (right) side of the court, until a winner of the set is decided.
  • Players change ends of the court after the first point and every four points thereafter (1, 5, 9, 13).
  • The set tiebreaker is first to 7 by 2.
  • The game and set score is now 7-6, so players change sides and the player who received for the first point of the tiebreaker serves for the first game of the second set.

Match tiebreak (third set): The match tiebreak (after each player has won 1 set) is treated as a third set. If the previous set ended in a tiebreaker, then the player who received for the first point of that tiebreaker will serve the first point of the match tiebreaker (first to 10 points by 2). Players change ends after the first point and every four points thereafter.

Score reporting format for tiebreakers: 7-6(7-3), 4-6, 1-0(10-6)

Tennis Balls

Both players should bring a new can of balls to the match. One can will be opened to play with. The winner takes home the unopened can and the loser takes home the opened can of used balls.

Sportsmanship

Remember that this ladder is for competition, but it's for fun. When making calls you should be 100% sure, or give the benefit of the doubt to your opponent. Be open to asking for your opponent's opinion on a call, and be friendly. It's OK to lose!

Score Reporting

After the match is over, both players should report the score right away on the website under "My Group". Here are the match outcomes that can be reported:

  • Completed — The match was played and there was a winner and a final score.
  • Schedule Problem — Both players were in communication trying to figure out a match time and day but couldn't match their schedules up.
  • Weather Problem — The weather made it so that the match could not be played.
  • Injury (match not finished) — The match was in the process of being played and one player had to retire due to an injury or sickness. The non-retiring player gets the win and the score is still reported with a comment on which player had which score.
  • Injury (match not played) — The match was never started due to an injury or sickness of one player. The non-injured player gets the win.
  • Forfeit — If one player tries hard to schedule a match and the other player makes no effort, the match is a forfeit. It's also a forfeit if one player just refuses or can't play that month unless it's due to injury. Write forfeit in for the score and the other player gets the win.

Monthly Rankings

At the beginning of the next month, the player with the best record in the group moves up to the next group, and the player with the worst record moves down a group. The middle player stays in the same spot. In the event of a tie in matches won, group movement will be decided first by head to head competition, then on sets won vs. sets lost. If it's still a tie at that point, the highest ranked player will move up and the lowest ranked player will move down.

Top 10 and bottom 10 shuffling: The top 10 spots and the bottom 10 spots work a little differently in order to mix things up for those players. For the top 10, at the end of the month: #1 stays at the top, #2 moves to #4, #3 moves to #7, #4 moves to #2, #5 stays at #5, #6 moves to #8, #7 moves to #3, #8 moves to #6, #9 moves to #10, #10 moves to #9. The bottom 10 move in a similar way only in reverse.

Please keep in mind that the ladder is very fluid month to month. Players can quit and rejoin at any time. Adding and subtracting players when the new ladder comes out on the 1st of the month can move things around quite a bit depending on the playing levels of the new players, as players are added near their own playing level. The primary goal of this free ladder is to have 2 competitive matches each month. The secondary goal is to try to move up the ladder.

I agree to follow all policies and rules, its office holders, agents and employees. I understand failure to comply with Texas Tennis Ladder's policies may result in my removal from said ladder.

Safety

Remember to be safe. If you are playing someone you don't know, it's always a good idea to play on courts where there are other people around or else bring someone along with you to your match.
